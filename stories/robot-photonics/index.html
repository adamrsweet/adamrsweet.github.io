Robot Photonics

Robots smaller than a grain of salt that live on light

I've written songs about mountains, rivers and forests. This one is about a robot you can barely see.

The research

In December 2025, engineers at the University of Pennsylvania and the University of Michigan announced the world's smallest fully programmable, autonomous robots. Each one is about 200 by 300 by 50 micrometers, smaller than a grain of salt. They swim. They carry tiny computers. They can sense the temperature around them and change course in response. They run on light from tiny solar cells, and under the glow of an LED they can keep swimming for months. Each one costs about a penny to make.

The researchers say robots like these could one day help monitor the health of individual cells, or help build very small devices.

Why I wrote it

Photonics, the science of light, is one of my favorite subjects to read about. What struck me here was how gentle it all sounds. No wires, no fuel, no joystick. Just light, warmth, and a machine that "decides" when to turn.

"Robot Photonics" is a song about those "little light walkers." I tried to get the details right, from the temperature sensing to the months of swimming to the Penn and Michigan labs.

What they said

"I think it's really impressive how you managed to incorporate so many of the details of this research project into the lyrics!"
— Ian Scheffler, Senior Science Writer

The bottom line

Science doesn't have to be loud to be amazing. Sometimes it's a speck of silicon swimming in the light, and it deserves a song.

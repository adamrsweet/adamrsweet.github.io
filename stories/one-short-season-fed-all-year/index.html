One Short Season, Fed All Year

A song for Kitchen Garden Farm in Sunderland, and the crew who stayed and bought the place

New England gives a farmer one short growing season. Kitchen Garden Farm makes it last all year.

What happened

Kitchen Garden Farm is a certified organic vegetable farm on South Silver Lane in Sunderland, with fields on both sides of the Connecticut River. Caroline Pam and Tim Wilcox started it in 2006 on one rented acre and grew it to 65 acres.

When it came time to step back, they didn't sell to a stranger. In 2024, two longtime employees, Lilly Israel and Max Traunstein, bought the farm. Both studied agriculture at UMass and had worked on the farm for close to a decade. The founders lowered the price so the people who knew the land best could keep it going.

The farm is known for specialty peppers and Italian vegetables like fennel, broccoli rabe and radicchio. Much of the harvest becomes fermented sriracha, fire-roasted salsa and giardiniera, so a July pepper can still be feeding someone in January.

Why I wrote the song

Every fall I wish my Granby garden could last longer. Kitchen Garden Farm actually does it, with patience, science and plain hard work. "Kitchen Garden Farm" follows the whole cycle, from summer fields to winter kitchens, and honors the people who chose to stay rooted and carry the work forward.

"What grows once feeds the whole year through / That is how they do it now."

How you can help

And put a bottle of their sriracha on your table. That helps more than any song.

The bottom line

Farms change hands every year in the Valley. Not many change hands this way, from the founders to the crew who grew up in its fields. That deserves a song.

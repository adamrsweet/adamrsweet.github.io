A Snack With a View

A trail song for the Wigwam on the Western Summit, where the summit treat is real

Every kid who ever climbed a mountain for a promised ice cream deserves this song.

In his Oct. 31 Hikes & Walks column in the Berkshire Eagle, Lauren R. Stevens remembered a camp hike in Maine where counselors promised soda and ice cream at the summit. There was no store. Then he described a hike where the reward is real: about three quarters of a mile and 800 feet of climbing on old logging and carting roads, up to the Wigwam gift shop on the Western Summit of Route 2, above North Adams. The Mansfield sisters opened the Wigwam in 1914 for the first automobile tourists. Kelly and Alan Scofield still welcome hikers today.

Why I wrote the song

I can't make that climb these days, but I could hear the song as soon as I read the column. "Wigwam Welcome" is built for walking, or for singing along in the car on Route 2.

"Three-quarter mile and eight hundred feet / A snack with a view at the end of the road."

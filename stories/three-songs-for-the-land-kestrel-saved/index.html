Three Songs for the Land Kestrel Saved

Pelham Hills, Spruce Hill, and the people who keep the Valley’s forests whole

Some organizations inspire one song. Kestrel Land Trust inspired three.

Pelham Hills

In October 2025, Kestrel and The Trustees announced the permanent protection of more than 2,000 acres of forest in Pelham and Belchertown. That includes the 1,094-acre Sarah Cowls Forest at Pelham Hills, in Pelham and Shutesbury, and Hop Brook Forest in Belchertown. The land stays forest for wildlife and clean water, with public access to sections of the Robert Frost Trail and the New England Scenic Trail.

I wrote "Pelham Hills" about sugar maples, old stone walls and the quiet brooks of those hills.

Spruce Hill, twice

In Westhampton, the town, Kestrel, Hilltown Land Trust and other partners set out to protect nearly 800 acres of forest, with help from a $1.25 million state grant. My first song, "Westhampton's Spruce Hill," is a sing-along about Rice Brook's cold water and trails for everyone.

In June 2026, the job was done. Kestrel announced that 806 acres are now permanently protected, growing a block of conserved forest to about 3,000 contiguous acres. Those streams help supply drinking water to Holyoke. That called for a second song, a slower one: "Spruce Hill Stands." "Long after we're gone these woods will remain / For children we'll never know."

What Kestrel said

"I'm so touched to hear that our work inspired you to create this beautiful music! … a moving reflection of the love of the land."
— Kari Blood, Community Engagement Director

The bottom line

Kestrel keeps the Valley's forests standing. Three songs are my way of saying thank you, and every download keeps that thank-you going.

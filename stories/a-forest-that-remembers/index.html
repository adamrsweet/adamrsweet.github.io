A Forest That Remembers

A song for Mohawk Trail State Forest, inspired by Rizwana Khan’s rainy-day walk

Some walks start with a wrong weather forecast and end somewhere much better.

What happened

In an Oct. 23 essay in the Amherst Indy, photographer and writer Rizwana Khan described a rainy day at Mohawk Trail State Forest: no cell signal, a rustic log cabin, and a clearing where the forest manager and two contractors were burning invasive honeysuckle so native plants could grow. The forest is known for old-growth hemlocks about 500 years old, red spruce about 400 years old, and white pines that are among the tallest trees in New England.

Why I wrote the song

Her essay stayed with me, especially her last line, which the song borrows for its ending: "The forest must open before light can reach the ground. What falls becomes nourishment in time, and in the quiet space left behind, life begins again." The song shares her title too, with thanks. It's a quiet song about renewal that doesn't erase what came before.

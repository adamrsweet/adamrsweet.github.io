Monadnock Remembers

A winter rescue on Ferry Spring Trail, and a summer climb with my son

Plenty of us in New England have climbed Mount Monadnock. It's the kind of mountain that feels friendly. On a cold December night, it wasn't.

The rescue

On Friday, December 19, 2025, around 10 p.m., New Hampshire Fish and Game conservation officers got word of hikers in trouble on the Ferry Spring Trail. Two young men had fallen into a brook. They were soaking wet and freezing, with high winds and dropping temperatures. They had no food, no water, no warm or dry clothes. A third hiker was trying to help them and stayed in touch by cell phone.

Conservation officers, with volunteers from the Upper Valley Wilderness Response Team, climbed up in the dark and reached them before 11 p.m. Then came the long, careful work of getting them down.

Why I wrote it

When I read the Fish and Game report, I thought of a different day on that same mountain. An August afternoon, blue sky, and my son Richard, nine years old, beside me on the summit rock. Boots too big, heart too brave, saying "Dad, we made it."

"Monadnock Remembers" holds both stories. It's about judgment and care, and about the people who climb up in the cold so the rest of us get to come home.

What they said

"We are very impressed it's a great song well done my friend! … we are sending it out to all of Field Lieutenants who do these missions!"
— Colonel Kevin J. Jordan, Chief of Law Enforcement

That one meant a lot. The song went to the people it was written for.

The bottom line

Before you hike, pack food, water, a light and a dry layer. And if you ever need them, the officers of Fish and Game will come up the mountain for you. This song is my thank-you.

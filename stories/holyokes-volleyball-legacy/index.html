Holyoke’s Volleyball Legacy

How a gym teacher in a mill city invented a game the whole world plays, and a song for the Hall that remembers him

Every beach game, every school gym, every Olympic final started in Holyoke.

What happened

In 1895, William G. Morgan was the 25-year-old physical director at the Holyoke YMCA. He'd been recruited to play football at Springfield by James Naismith, the man who invented basketball a few years earlier just down the road. Morgan saw that basketball was too strenuous for many of the older men who came to his gym. So he set out to invent something gentler.

He called it "Mintonette." The first game was played that December. It didn't look much like today's game. There was no limit on players, and it was played in innings. Before long it got a better name: volleyball.

By 1964 volleyball was an Olympic sport. In 1985, Morgan became the first inductee of the Volleyball Hall of Fame. Two years later, the Hall opened its doors at 444 Dwight Street in Holyoke, the city where it all began.

Why I wrote the song

"Holyoke's Volleyball Legacy" is a tribute to the mill workers of Holyoke and the quiet invention that changed the world. It goes from factory floors and lunch-break games to Olympic arenas. I love that one of the world's great sports began as a kindness: a game where everybody could play.

"From milltown grit to global call / Holyoke gave the world its ball."

How you can help

If you've never been, visit the Hall on Dwight Street. It's a reminder that big things can start in small Western Mass places.

The bottom line

Holyoke gave the world a game built on teamwork. The Hall of Fame keeps that story home. A song is my way of cheering for both.

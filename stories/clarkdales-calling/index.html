Clarkdale’s Calling

A song for a Deerfield orchard that tells its own story, one pause at a time

Some farms sell you apples. Clarkdale also tells you their story.

What happened

Clarkdale Fruit Farms on Upper Road in Deerfield is a fourth-generation family orchard. Some of its heirloom apple trees were planted by the first generation of Clarks more than a century ago.

In late August 2025, co-owner Lori Holmes Clark opened something new: "Doorways: Mirth and Memory at Clarkdale Fruit Farms," a 90-minute audio walk through the orchard. She calls it a "sound pool." You put on headphones, open the free ECHOES app, and wander. Voices and songs rise up at 27 "pauses."

Tom Clark tells the story of the oldest trees. Ben Clark talks about grafting, deer fencing, disease, and the hail and wind he ties to a changing climate. Their son Emerson, who was 11 when he recorded his part, talks about the life in a single drop of water. Friends read poems, local musicians like Kris Delmhorst fill in the rest, and fairy houses hide under the oldest apple tree.

Lori put it simply: "This is [the farmers'] art, and their art literally feeds people."

Why I wrote the song

I loved the idea of a farm that asks you to slow down and listen. "Clarkdale's Calling" walks the same path as the tour, from the first trees to the fairy houses. The chorus says it all: "Clarkdale's calling, come roam with me."

How you can help

And visit. Bring a charged phone and headphones, buy a bag of apples, and take the walk.

The bottom line

Family farms like Clarkdale are why the Valley still tastes like the Valley. A song is a small thank-you. The bigger one is showing up and buying the fruit.

The Turning Line

A song for the UMass Sunwheel, where neighbors gather among standing stones to watch the seasons turn

Just south of McGuirk Stadium in Amherst, there's a circle of standing stones. Four times a year, people show up at dawn to watch the sun.

What happened

The UMass Amherst Sunwheel lines up with the places on the horizon where the sun rises and sets at the solstices and equinoxes. This year marks 29 years of public Sunwheel gatherings. At each one, UMass astronomers explain why the sun's rising point moves through the year, where the Earth, sun and moon sit in each season, and answer the public's questions. Professor Stephen Schneider has led many of these talks over the years.

The next gathering is the winter solstice, Monday, December 21, 2026: sunrise at 7:00 am and sunset at 3:30 pm. Dress for the cold and wet ground. Heavy rain or snow cancels.

Why I wrote the song

When I read the university's notice for the fall equinox, I wrote a song about it. "The Turning Line" imagines standing inside the circle at the equinox, watching the western stone as the light crosses over. No hand moves the marker. It's only the Earth, turning, "through an old geometry." On Bandcamp it's credited to Dál Riata Còmhlan.

"Equal light, equal night / East to west, the turning line / Equal light, equal night / For a moment, we align."

It's about the equinox, but the feeling carries to the solstice: neighbors in a cold field, remembering we live on a turning planet.

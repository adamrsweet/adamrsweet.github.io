Pickle Bot

A work song for the people who unload the trucks, and the small MIT crew building a robot to spare their backs

Almost everything you buy rides in the back of a truck. Somebody has to carry it out.

What happened

In 2018, three MIT alumni, AJ Meyer, Ariana Eisenstein and Dan Paluska, went to a UPS warehouse and watched 15 people unload trucks through a winter night shift. It's some of the hardest work there is. Boxes are heavy, trailers can be freezing or baking hot, and warehouse injury rates run more than twice the national average.

So they started the Pickle Robot Company, now based in Charlestown, Massachusetts. Their one-armed robots roll into a trailer and unload it on their own, picking up boxes of up to 50 pounds and setting them on a conveyor belt. The name is a nod to the Apple Computer Company. Early on, money was running low, and their first robot could only unload reliably for about 20 seconds at a time. They posted a video of it anyway, and the company grew from there.

MIT News told their story on December 5, 2025.

Why I wrote the song

"Pickle Bot" was born on a long, hot loading dock. It's a tribute to the folks who haul the heavy stuff, and to the small crew that dared to build a machine to spare some backs.

It's a song about work, about handing off the dull pain of drudgery, and about how one robot arm can point toward a better deal for everyone.

How you can help

And next time a package lands on your porch, think about the people who lifted it.

The bottom line

Good machines should take the worst jobs, so people can do better ones. Pickle is building toward that, right here in Massachusetts.

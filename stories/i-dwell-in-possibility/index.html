I Dwell in Possibility

A New Year song for the Emily Dickinson Museum, and for the house where her words still live

On New Year's Day, the Emily Dickinson Museum sent out a few of her lines: "I dwell in Possibility – / A fairer House than Prose – / More numerous of Windows –." I couldn't stop thinking about them.

What happened

The museum on Main Street in Amherst is made up of two homes: the Homestead, where Dickinson was born, did most of her writing, and died, and The Evergreens, her brother Austin's house next door.

The museum has been bringing the Homestead back to the way it looked in her writing years. This spring it put on a new cedar roof and began the research for the last phase of restoration: the kitchen where she baked her gingerbread, the pantry, and the rooms where the household's work was done. It's careful, slow work, like archaeology inside a house.

Why I wrote the song

"I Dwell In Positivity" is my answer to that New Year message. It draws on Dickinson's own words and her sense of imagination, and on the idea of hope carried forward through time. It looks ahead to a new year shaped by care, learning, restoration and shared responsibility.

"A voice once quiet, radical, clear / Still meets the moment year by year."

How you can help

You can also stop by on Saturday, December 12, for the museum's open house celebrating her 196th birthday, with tours, music, hot cider and gingerbread.

The bottom line

Emily Dickinson lived her whole life just down the road from Granby. Her house is still standing, and her windows are still open. That's worth a song, and worth a few dollars.

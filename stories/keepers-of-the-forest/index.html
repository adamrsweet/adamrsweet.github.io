New Song: Keepers of the Forest

One of the themes that runs through my Songs for the Land series is the idea that conservation is ultimately an act of hope.

Recently, I read about the protection of more than 380 acres of forestland in Sandisfield. The land sits between existing state forests and helps create a larger connected landscape for wildlife while protecting important water resources and public access opportunities.

Stories like this rarely make national headlines, yet they matter enormously. Forests do more than provide scenery. They filter water, store carbon, support biodiversity, and create space for people to experience the natural world. When a parcel of land is protected, the benefits often continue for generations.

That idea became the inspiration for “Keepers of the Forest.”

The song is not about government agencies or policies. It is about stewardship. It is about recognizing that every generation inherits a landscape shaped by the decisions of those who came before. We enjoy forests, rivers, and wildlife today because someone chose to protect them yesterday.

As part of the Songs for the Land series, this piece celebrates the people, organizations, and communities that work to conserve the places that make western Massachusetts special. Their efforts help ensure that future generations will still be able to walk these woods, hear birdsong in the trees, and experience the beauty of a living landscape.

Sometimes the most important legacy we leave behind is simply a place left wild.

New Song: Halifax Bay

Halifax Bay

History is full of moments when ordinary decisions lead to extraordinary consequences. The Halifax Explosion of December 6, 1917, was one of those moments.

Two ships met in the narrow entrance to Halifax Harbour. One of them, the French cargo ship Mont-Blanc, carried a massive load of wartime explosives. A collision led to a fire, and the fire led to an explosion so powerful that it destroyed much of the city and claimed nearly two thousand lives.

When I read about the disaster, I was struck not only by the scale of the destruction but by the human story behind it. Sailors making decisions in a crowded channel. Citizens watching a burning ship from the waterfront. Families whose lives changed in an instant.

“Halifax Bay” tells that story the way sailors working together aboard a ship would have.

Sea chanties were often used to coordinate labor, but they also preserved stories. This song is my attempt to remember one of the most significant maritime disasters in history and the people whose lives were affected by it.

More than a century later, the lessons remain the same. Respect the sea. Respect the risks. And remember those who came before us.

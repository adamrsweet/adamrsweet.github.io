Sheep, Bees, and Leeks: A Song for Upinngil Farm in Gill

The barnyard theory of inevitability, and a farm that keeps changing

Some farms start with a grand plan. Upinngil Farm in Gill started with a small one: sheep for the wool, bees for the honey, and leeks by the crate for the kitchen.

That was Clifford and Patricia Hatch’s idea back in the late ‘80s, on a hillside in Gill. It didn’t stay that simple. The leeks didn’t sell the way Clifford hoped, but people loved picking strawberries in the summer. So the farm followed the people. Then came cows and raw milk, then cheese, then a bakery and a farm store that’s open all year. The family has a name for it: the barnyard theory of inevitability.

What I love about this story is that nobody forced it. They planted what they knew, watched what their neighbors wanted, and kept changing. Their daughter Sorrel came home from college with ideas about farming more kindly, Isaac built the buildings, and the farm kept growing into something nobody planned at the start.

The chorus says it for me:

“Oh, sheep, bees, and leeks, that was the plan,

But the barnyard’s got a mind of its own, my friend.”

It’s a song about changing gears, and about a corner of the Pioneer Valley where people show up for each other.

If you’re up near Gill, stop by the farm store and say hello for me.

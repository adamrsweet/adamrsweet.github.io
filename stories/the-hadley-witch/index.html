The Hadley Witch and Our Local Public Radio

A 340-year-old Hadley story, a song, and a promise to NEPM

Most of us in the Valley have heard of Half-Hanged Mary. Few of us know how close to home her story sits.

What happened in Hadley

Mary Reeve married William Webster, and they lived poor at the edge of Hadley. In 1683 she was sent to Boston and tried for witchcraft. The jury acquitted her, and she came home to the same neighbors.

That winter of 1684-85, Philip Smith, a deacon and a judge, fell sick and blamed her. A group of young men dragged Mary out of her house, hanged her from a tree, rolled her in the snow, and buried her in it. In the morning she was still alive. Smith died that winter. Mary lived about eleven more years. Cotton Mather wrote it up as proof of witchcraft, which is a big part of why we still know her name.

Why I wrote the song

"The Hadley Witch" tells her story here in Granby, a few miles from where it happened. I tried not to make her a cartoon witch or a saint.

I sent the song to Monte Belmonte, who hosts The Fabulous 413 on 88.5 NEPM with Kaliis Smith.

Here's what Monte wrote back:

"This song is very interesting… We'll be featuring this story on the show today."
— Monte Belmonte, The Fabulous 413

The Fabulous 413 calls itself a kind of "Mister Rogers' Neighborhood" for grown-ups. Shows like it keep stories like Mary's alive.

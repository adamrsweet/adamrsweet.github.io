Amherst Swing

A song for Fred Tillis, a jazz afternoon at Wildwood, and a town that loves its music

Not many cemeteries throw a jazz concert. Wildwood did, and it inspired the happiest song I've written in a while.

A jazz afternoon at Wildwood

Wildwood Cemetery was established in 1887, with a landscape inspired by the work of Frederick Law Olmsted. It's a quiet hilltop in the middle of Amherst, and it's also the final resting place of Dr. Fred Tillis: composer, poet, educator, and for almost 20 years the leader of the UMass Fine Arts Center.

On June 22, 2025, Wildwood held its first "Wildwood Honors" concert to mark what would have been Fred Tillis's 95th birthday. His former students and colleagues played his music: Royal Hartigan on drums, Charles Langford on saxophone, Wes Brown on bass and Alexis Marcelo on piano.

Why I wrote the song

I read about the concert in the Daily Hampshire Gazette's "Around Amherst" column. "Amherst Swing" honors Fred's legacy, and it tips its hat to the Giant Book Sale and the other things that make Amherst feel like home.

What they said

"This song is so much fun!!!! … We are always looking for donations so your offer is very much appreciated."
— Rebecca, Friends of Wildwood Cemetery

The Friends of Wildwood Cemetery work to restore and care for Wildwood's historic grounds and natural beauty, and to bring people there for events like this one.

The bottom line

A cemetery full of jazz is a good reminder that how we remember people matters. Wildwood remembers Fred Tillis with music, and this song is a small way to help.

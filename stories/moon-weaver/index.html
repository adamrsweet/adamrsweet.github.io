Moon Weaver

A song for Jared Isaacman, and the last track on Past the Sky

Yes, the title tips its hat to Gary Wright. "Dream Weaver" came out in 1975, and it has stayed with me for fifty years. When I started writing about Jared Isaacman, the kid who told his kindergarten teacher he was going to space, the word "weaver" wouldn't leave me alone.

The story

Jared left high school at 16, built a company in his parents' basement, learned to fly, became the first private citizen to walk in space, and now leads NASA as it gets ready to go back to the Moon. I wanted the song to move the way that life did: patient at first, then lifting.

My favorite lines are in the bridge:

"We left our footprints, then we left them behind
But the Moon never moved and the dream never died"

How it sits with my other space songs

Moon Weaver closes Past the Sky, my album of songs about NASA, the people who fly, and the machines we send up. Every song on it starts with a real story.

  • Come Lift Her Again: Hubble is slipping toward the atmosphere, and NASA is asking who can boost her back up.
  • Old 3I: the interstellar comet 3I/ATLAS, older than our Sun, carrying ice from before we had stars.
  • The Barrier's Been Breached: the May 2024 solar storm that tore a hole in the Van Allen belt.
  • Four Minutes: two bursts of light the Swift telescope caught four minutes apart, from the same patch of sky.
  • A Romantic Idea: did comets really bring the seeds of life to Earth? Probably not, and the song says so gently.
  • The Hidden One ED138: a small world past Pluto that the Rubin telescope found hiding in old pictures.
  • The Chartreuse Zombie Star: a dead star glowing green after swallowing its planets.

Moon Weaver is the odd one out. It's the only song about a person instead of a discovery, the longest by far at five and a half minutes. The others are news ballads. This one is a portrait.

If it carries you somewhere, pass it to someone who still looks up.

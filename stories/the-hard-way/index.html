The Hard Way

A song for two libraries, Housatonic’s Ramsdell and Amherst’s Jones

Two towns, one familiar melody of good intentions meeting hard municipal reality.

In the village of Housatonic, the 1908 Ramsdell Public Library is one of Great Barrington's few town-owned buildings on the National Register of Historic Places. Its renovation has stalled under pressures Amherst knows well from the Jones Library: state grant requirements pushing ambitious designs, historic preservation questions arriving late, no total cost estimate, and private fundraising hopes fading away.

Great Barrington also knows how to keep a community space. In the early 1920s, local Jewish farmers, cattlemen and shopkeepers founded Congregation Ahavath Sholom ("Love of Peace") and raised the money for their schoolhouse building with a klezmer concert. It is still the oldest continuously used synagogue building in Berkshire County.

Why I wrote the song

"The Hard Way" opens with a slow doina, a clarinet alone, like someone walking the empty stacks of a library whose future is still being argued. Then it moves into a freylekh that keeps circling the same phrase without quite resolving. It isn't a protest song, and it isn't a joke. It's the sound of towns that keep learning that history doesn't step aside just because the drawings are finished.

"Ramsdell, Ramsdell / Historic and waiting."

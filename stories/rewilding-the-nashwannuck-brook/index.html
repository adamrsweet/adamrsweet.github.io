Rewilding the Nashwannuck Brook

How Northampton is turning an old golf course back into a living stream

Golf courses are built to drain water away fast. Northampton is teaching one to hold water again.

From fairway to floodplain

For about sixty years, a 105-acre piece of land on Old Wilson Road was the Pine Grove Golf Course. Over that time, Nashawannuck Brook was turned from a meandering stream with wetlands around it into a straight channel that hurries water and sediment away.

The city bought the land in 2020. Since then, Northampton and its partners, including the Massachusetts Division of Ecological Restoration and Mass Audubon, have been putting it back. They're replanting forest, removing old structures, and giving the brook back a natural channel and wetlands. That means more room to store water in big storms, less erosion and downstream flooding, and more carbon held in the ground. The first construction phase, including removing a failed culvert at Old Wilson Road, wrapped up this summer, and the city has since won a grant for the next phase.

Why I wrote it

I loved the idea of a town choosing to step aside and let the land remember what it was. The song follows that path: pulled drain tiles, broken pipes, log jams set "like patient hands," and a heron drifting in.

What they said

"I'm so grateful that the Nashawannuck Brook project in particular has elicited a connection and creative response… Like 'Seminole Wind,' but opposite!"
— Sarah LaValley, Northampton Office of Planning and Sustainability

"Seminole Wind" is John Anderson's song about the draining of the Florida Everglades. I'll take "the opposite" as high praise.

The bottom line

Sometimes the best thing we can do for a place is undo what we did to it. Northampton is doing that, one log jam at a time.
